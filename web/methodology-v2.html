Methodology

How Warren researches a company.

Warren is an investment research analyst persona. The product is designed to help you understand the strongest evidence for and against an investment—not to imitate Warren Buffett or manufacture a precise “correct” price.

Ask Warren separates structured financial computation from narrative analysis. Reproducible metrics are calculated from structured data; the analysis layer explains what those facts may mean, challenges the thesis, and traces important conclusions back to sources.

What Warren evaluates

Business quality

Margins, cash generation, returns, resilience, capital allocation, and evidence of durable competitive strength.

Growth & expectations

Revenue and earnings growth, analyst estimates, estimate revisions, recent surprises, and forward-looking evidence.

Valuation context

Common valuation ratios, free-cash-flow yield, current market expectations, and the analyst price-target range.

Risk & evidence quality

Execution, financial, regulatory, competitive and demand risks, plus the completeness and authority of retrieved sources.

Analyst price targets, not an Ask Warren fair-value forecast

Ask Warren uses the reported analyst low, median, and high price targets as one input into the research view. It does not present a proprietary DCF value as a price prediction in the user experience.

The research workflow

1

Collect structured company data

Market data, financial statements, analyst estimates, revisions, earnings history, technical context, and other structured inputs.

2

Retrieve evidence

Primary filing material, filing passages, recent headlines, earnings-call analyst Q&A when available, and selected macro or web research.

3

Build competing cases

The Bull and Bear perspectives independently identify the strongest supported arguments rather than forcing a single narrative too early.

4

Evaluate the evidence

Warren weighs business quality, expectations, valuation context, risks, source strength, and missing information.

5

State the view and what could change it

The result is a three-state research verdict, confidence level, strongest reasons to own or wait, and observable signals that could change the thesis.

Warren's three research verdicts

Attractive

The current evidence suggests a favorable balance between business quality, expectations, valuation context, and risk.

Watch

The company may be strong, but the evidence, expectations, price, or uncertainty do not yet support an attractive conclusion.

Avoid

The current evidence points to an unfavorable risk/reward or a materially weakened investment case.

Evidence discipline

  • Retrieved filing metadata, structured XBRL facts, filing passages, news headlines, estimates, and transcript excerpts are treated as different evidence types.
  • Headlines are not treated as if the underlying article was fully read.
  • Missing or partial sources reduce confidence instead of silently disappearing.
  • Important supported arguments expose source links so the user can inspect the evidence.
  • Unsupported filler is not added merely to reach a fixed number of Bull or Bear points.

Limits

Analyst targets can be stale, biased, clustered, or wrong. Market-data providers can return incomplete fields. News headlines lack full article context. Filing retrieval may miss relevant passages. Model-generated synthesis can make reasoning mistakes. Ask Warren should therefore be used as a research aid, with important conclusions verified against primary sources.

Research software only. Ask Warren does not provide personalized investment advice, guarantee returns, or execute trades.
